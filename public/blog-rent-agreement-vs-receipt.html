Rent Agreement vs Rent Receipt: Key Legal Differences, State Stamp Duty & HR Proofs

Authored by RentReceipt.online Legal Editorial Desk • Validated for FY 2025-26 & FY 2026-27 • Last Updated: November 4, 2026

During corporate tax submission windows between December and February, human resource portals across India buzz with confusion over documentation requirements. Employees frequently ask: "Do I need to submit a registered rent agreement, or are monthly rent receipts alone sufficient to claim my House Rent Allowance (HRA) under Section 10(13A)?"

While both documents pertain to the same rented premises, they serve entirely distinct statutory and evidentiary functions under Indian law. Understanding the legal difference is crucial to preventing tax disallowances and audit notices.

The Legal Framework of Rent Agreements

Under Section 105 of the Transfer of Property Act, 1882, a lease of immovable property is a transfer of a right to enjoy such property, made for a certain time in consideration of a price paid or promised.

The 11-Month Convention in India: Under Section 17(1)(d) of the Registration Act, 1908, any lease of immovable property from year to year or for any term exceeding one year must be compulsorily registered with the Sub-Registrar of Assurances. To avoid the high stamp duty, registration charges, and cumbersome in-person registration procedures associated with long-term leases, landlords and tenants routinely execute 11-month Leave and License agreements, which can be executed on non-judicial stamp paper without mandatory registrar visits in most states (except Maharashtra, which mandates e-registration for all tenancies).

State-by-State Stamp Duty Rates for Tenancy Agreements

Stamp duty on tenancy agreements varies significantly across Indian states:

  • Maharashtra: Mandates compulsory digital registration (e-registration) for all Leave and License agreements. Stamp duty is calculated at 0.25% of the total rent plus refundable deposit for the entire duration, along with a flat ₹1,000 registration fee (urban).
  • Karnataka (Bengaluru): Typically executed on ₹100 or ₹200 e-stamp paper for agreements under 11 months, with stamp duty of 0.5% to 1% for registered longer-term leases.
  • Delhi: Standard 11-month agreements are executed on ₹100 stamp paper. Leases of 1 to 5 years attract 2% stamp duty on average annual rent.
  • Tamil Nadu: Standard 11-month agreements utilize ₹100 or ₹200 non-judicial stamp paper.
  • Uttar Pradesh (Noida/Ghaziabad): Short-term tenancy agreements are executed on ₹100 to ₹500 non-judicial stamp paper.

The Legal Role of Rent Receipts

While a rent agreement proves you have agreed to rent a property, it does not prove that you actually paid the money. A tenant might execute a lease for ₹30,000/month, but vacate the flat after two months or default on payment.

This is why the Income Tax Department and corporate employers require Monthly Rent Receipts. Under Section 10(13A), HRA exemption is strictly predicated on actual expenditure incurred.

Revenue Stamp Nuances Under the Indian Stamp Act

Under Schedule I, Article 53 of the Indian Stamp Act, 1899:

  • A receipt for any money or other property the amount or value of which exceeds ₹5,000 must be stamped with a ₹1 revenue stamp if paid in cash.
  • The landlord must sign across the face of the revenue stamp so that the signature partially covers both the stamp and the paper. An unstamped cash receipt exceeding ₹5,000 is inadmissible as evidence of payment in a civil court under Section 35 of the Stamp Act.
  • Electronic Transfer Exemption: When rent is transferred via verifiable digital banking channels (UPI, NEFT, IMPS, RTGS, or Account Payee Cheque), the transaction reference and bank statement serve as independent proof, rendering physical revenue stamps non-mandatory under CBDT guidelines.

Why Employers Require Both Documents

Enterprise payroll portals (such as Darwinbox, Keka, GreytHR, and ZingHR) typically operate on this verification logic:

  1. Rent under ₹3,000/month: Exemption permitted without rent receipts or agreements (as per CBDT basic limits).
  2. Rent between ₹3,000 and ₹8,333/month: Monthly rent receipts with landlord signature required.
  3. Rent between ₹8,333 and ₹25,000/month: Monthly rent receipts with mandatory Landlord PAN required.
  4. Rent exceeding ₹25,000/month: Both the registered Rent Agreement AND all 12 monthly rent receipts with Landlord PAN are required.
