Paying Rent to Parents to Save Tax: ITAT Rulings, § 24 Deductions & Audit Checklist

Authored by RentReceipt.online Tax Editorial Team • Validated for FY 2025-26 & FY 2026-27 • Last Updated: November 4, 2026

For millions of salaried young professionals living with their parents in major metropolitan hubs like Bengaluru, Mumbai, Delhi-NCR, Hyderabad, and Pune, paying rent to parents is a legitimate and highly tax-effective financial planning strategy.

Under Section 10(13A) of the Income Tax Act, 1961, an employee can claim exemption on House Rent Allowance (HRA) if they actually incur expenditure on rent for residential premises occupied by them. Nothing in the statutory text of the Income Tax Act forbids paying rent to family members—provided the transaction is genuine, executed at arm's length, and fully substantiated by a verifiable banking trail.

The Landmark Legal Precedent: Bajrang Prasad Gupta vs. ITO

The landmark authority establishing the legality of paying rent to parents comes from the Income Tax Appellate Tribunal (ITAT), Ahmedabad Bench in the case of Bajrang Prasad Gupta vs. ITO (ITAT Ahmedabad, ITA No. 129/Ahd/2012).

In this case, the Assessing Officer (AO) had disallowed the taxpayer's HRA claim on the grounds that paying rent to parents while living in the same family home was a sham transaction intended merely to evade tax. On appeal, the ITAT decisively overturned the disallowance, ruling that:

"Where the parents are legal owners of the residential property, and the assessee has actually made rent payments through account-payee cheques or bank transfers, and where the parents have declared the rental income in their respective returns of income, the expenditure cannot be questioned merely on account of family relationship."

Similar positions have been reaffirmed in subsequent rulings by the ITAT Mumbai and Delhi benches, establishing firm legal grounding for salaried taxpayers who follow procedural discipline.

The 5 Non-Negotiable Conditions for Legitimate Parent HRA Claims

To withstand automated AIS cross-verification and scrutiny notices from the Income Tax Department, your parent rental arrangement must satisfy five mandatory conditions:

  1. Exclusive Legal Ownership by Parents: The residential house property must be registered in the name of your father, mother, or both. You (the employee claiming HRA) must not be a co-owner or joint titleholder of the property. If you own even a fractional share of the property, claiming HRA for renting your own asset is invalid under Section 10(13A).
  2. Formal Rent Agreement Executed: A formal tenancy agreement should be drafted on non-judicial stamp paper as per your state’s stamp duty rate. The agreement should clearly identify your parent as the landlord/lessor, you as the tenant/lessee, the monthly rent amount, security deposit details, and the rented premises description.
  3. Verifiable Electronic Banking Trail: Do not pay parent rent in untraceable cash. Execute monthly transfers via NetBanking, NEFT, IMPS, or UPI. The transaction remarks should clearly read "Rent for [Month Year]". This bank statement trail is your primary defense during scrutiny.
  4. Issued Monthly Rent Receipts: Generate formal monthly rent receipts signed by your parent. Under CBDT rules, if annual rent exceeds ₹1,00,000, your parent's PAN must be stated on each receipt and on Form 12BB submitted to your employer.
  5. Parent Must Report Rental Income in ITR: Your parent must declare the total rent received as 'Income from House Property' on their annual Income Tax Return (ITR-1 or ITR-2). Because employer Form 24Q data feeds directly into the parent’s Annual Information Statement (AIS), omitting this income triggers immediate automated notices under Section 142(1).
⚠️ Crucial Warning: Why You Cannot Pay Rent to Your Spouse

While paying rent to parents is legally sound, paying rent to your husband or wife is strictly disallowed under Indian tax law. Under Section 64 of the Income Tax Act (clubbing of income provisions), any income transferred directly or indirectly to a spouse is clubbed back into the taxpayer's total income. Furthermore, courts have ruled that marriage creates a joint matrimonial obligation of cohabitation, invalidating a landlord-tenant relationship between spouses.
