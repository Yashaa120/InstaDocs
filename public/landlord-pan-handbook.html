Landlord PAN Compliance Mandate & Tenant Legal Rights Handbook

Authored by RentReceipt.online Legal Editorial Team • Updated for FY 2025-26 & FY 2026-27 • Last Audited: November 4, 2026

One of the most persistent points of friction between tenants and residential property owners in India centers on the mandatory disclosure of the Landlord’s Permanent Account Number (PAN) for House Rent Allowance (HRA) tax exemption claims.

Many landlords express reluctance or refuse outright to share their PAN card details, fearing increased tax liability or audit scrutiny. Conversely, salaried tenants who fail to furnish their landlord’s PAN face severe tax penalties, including the complete disallowance of their HRA exemption and immediate deductions of heavy TDS by employer payroll departments.

The Statutory Mandate: CBDT Circular No. 08/2013

The requirement to obtain and report a landlord's PAN is not an arbitrary rule created by corporate HR teams; it is a binding statutory mandate issued by the Central Board of Direct Taxes (CBDT).

Under CBDT Circular No. 08/2013 (reiterated in annual withholding tax circulars issued under Section 192 of the Income Tax Act, 1961), an employee claiming HRA must report the landlord’s PAN to the employer if annual rent paid exceeds ₹1,00,000 (which translates to approximately ₹8,333.33 per month).

Annual Rent Range Monthly Rent Threshold Landlord PAN Requirement
Up to ₹1,00,000 Up to ₹8,333 / month EXEMPT: No landlord PAN is required. Rent receipts with landlord signature suffice.
₹1,00,001 to ₹6,00,000 ₹8,334 to ₹50,000 / month MANDATORY: Landlord PAN must be quoted on rent receipts and Form 12BB.
Exceeding ₹6,00,000 Exceeding ₹50,000 / month MANDATORY + 2% TDS: Tenant must deduct 2% TDS under Section 194-IB and file Form 26QC.

What If Your Landlord Does Not Possess a PAN Card?

In situations where a landlord is an elderly individual, a pensioner, or an agriculturist whose total annual income falls below the minimum basic exemption limit (₹2,50,000 under the Old Regime or ₹3,00,000 under the New Regime), they may not have applied for or been allotted a PAN card.

In this scenario, CBDT guidelines provide an official legal alternative:

  1. The landlord must complete and sign a statutory declaration in Form 60 (under Rule 114B of the Income Tax Rules).
  2. The tenant must obtain a signed statement containing the landlord’s full legal name, residential address, property ownership verification, and an affirmation that their annual taxable income is below the exemption ceiling.
  3. This Form 60 declaration is submitted to the employer's payroll team alongside monthly rent receipts in lieu of a PAN card.

What If the Landlord Refuses to Provide Their PAN?

If your landlord earns taxable income but deliberately refuses to provide their PAN card to evade declaring rental income, consider these practical and legal remedies:

  • Incorporate PAN Disclosure into the Lease Deed: Before signing any lease agreement, insist on an explicit clause requiring the landlord to provide a copy of their PAN card and execute monthly rent receipts as an obligation of tenancy.
  • Direct ITR Claim with Full Bank Trails: If your employer disallows HRA due to a missing PAN, do not panic. Pay your rent exclusively through electronic banking channels (UPI, NEFT, or NetBanking). When you file your annual ITR-1 or ITR-2 before July 31, claim your eligible Section 10(13A) deduction directly. You can provide your landlord's name, property address, and bank statement trail if requested during e-verification.
  • Form 10BA under Section 80GG: If you are completely unable to substantiate HRA through your employer, you may explore whether claiming deductions under Section 80GG (up to ₹60,000/year) applies to your tax profile.

High-Value Tenancies: Mandatory Section 194-IB TDS

Tenants paying luxury or executive rents must be aware of an additional statutory withholding tax obligation.

Under Section 194-IB of the Income Tax Act, any individual or Hindu Undivided Family (HUF) paying monthly rent exceeding ₹50,000 to a resident landlord must deduct Tax Deducted at Source (TDS).

Important Rate Revision (Finance Act 2024):

Prior to October 1, 2024, the TDS rate under Section 194-IB was 5%. Under the revised Finance Act, the TDS rate has been reduced to 2% to ease administrative burdens. If the landlord does not furnish a PAN, TDS must be deducted at 20% under Section 206AA (capped at the final month's rent).

How to Execute Section 194-IB TDS:

  • You do not need a corporate Tax Deduction Account Number (TAN). The deduction is executed using your personal PAN.
  • TDS must be deducted once per financial year—either in the last month of the tenancy or in March.
  • Deposit the deducted TDS online using challan-cum-statement Form 26QC within 30 days from the end of the month in which deduction was made.
  • Download and issue Form 16C (the TDS certificate) to your landlord within 15 days of filing Form 26QC.

AIS & TIS Cross-Verification: How Tax Authorities Detect Mismatches

With the implementation of the Annual Information Statement (AIS) and Taxpayer Information Summary (TIS), the Income Tax Department's automated algorithms cross-verify data reported across tax filings:

  • When an employer files quarterly Form 24Q reporting employee HRA claims with landlord PANs, the rental payment data is automatically populated into the landlord's AIS profile under 'Rent Received'.
  • If the landlord fails to report this corresponding income under 'Income from House Property' on their ITR-1 or ITR-2, automated compliance notices under Section 142(1) or Section 133(6) are triggered.
