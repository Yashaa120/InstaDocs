House Rent Allowance (HRA) is one of the most effective tax-saving allowances provided to salaried employees in India. Under Section 10(13A) of the Income Tax Act, 1961, read in conjunction with Rule 2A of the Income Tax Rules, 1962, the entire HRA component received from an employer is not automatically tax-free.

Instead, the exact exempt portion must be mathematically computed by evaluating three statutory criteria. Any HRA received in excess of the calculated exemption is treated as taxable salary income.

The Three Statutory Criteria Under Rule 2A

Under Rule 2A, the amount of HRA exempt from income tax is the minimum (least) of the following three parameters:

Statutory Exemption Formula (Rule 2A):
Exempt HRA = MIN of:
1. Actual HRA Received: Total House Rent Allowance disbursed by the employer during the financial year.
2. City Percentage of Salary:
  • 50% of Basic Salary + DA: If the rented residence is in a designated Metro City (Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai).
  • 40% of Basic Salary + DA: For all other Indian cities (including Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune, Gurugram, Noida, Ahmedabad).
3. Excess Rent Over Base: Actual rent paid minus 10% of (Basic Salary + DA).

What Constitutes "Salary" for HRA Purposes?

In common corporate parlance, employees often confuse Gross CTC with the statutory definition of "Salary" used for income tax calculations. Under Explanation to Rule 2A, "Salary" for HRA computation is strictly defined as:

  • Basic Salary: The core contractual basic wage.
  • Dearness Allowance (DA): Only to the extent it enters into retirement benefits (pension/gratuity) as per terms of employment.
  • Commission: Fixed percentage commissions achieved on turnover, if contractually stipulated.
  • Excluded: Special allowances, conveyance allowance, medical allowances, performance bonuses, and employer PF contributions are strictly excluded from the HRA salary base.

The Metro vs. Non-Metro Classification Nuance

A frequent misconception among tech employees in Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Gurugram, and Pune is assuming these booming IT hubs qualify for the 50% metro tier. Under Indian income tax law:

  • Only Four Historic Metros Qualify for 50%: Greater Mumbai, Delhi (NCT), Kolkata, and Chennai.
  • All Other Cities Are 40%: Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune, Ahmedabad, Gurugram, Noida, Kochi, and Chandigarh fall strictly into the 40% tier, despite high rental prices.

Step-by-Step Worked Numerical Calculation

Consider Ananya, a software engineer residing in Bengaluru (40% non-metro) with the following annual payroll figures:

  • Annual Basic Salary: ₹6,00,000 (₹50,000/month)
  • Annual HRA Received from Employer: ₹2,40,000 (₹20,000/month)
  • Actual Rent Paid to Landlord: ₹3,00,000 (₹25,000/month)

Applying Rule 2A:

  1. Condition 1 (Actual HRA Received): ₹2,40,000
  2. Condition 2 (40% of Basic Salary): 40% of ₹6,00,000 = ₹2,40,000
  3. Condition 3 (Rent Paid minus 10% of Basic Salary): ₹3,00,000 − (10% of ₹6,00,000) = ₹3,00,000 − ₹60,000 = ₹2,40,000

The minimum of the three amounts is ₹2,40,000. Consequently, Ananya's entire HRA is 100% tax-exempt! At a 30% tax bracket, this statutory exemption saves her ₹74,880 in net annual income tax.
