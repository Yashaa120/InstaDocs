Section 80GG Rent Deduction: How Self-Employed & Non-HRA Employees Save Up to ₹60,000

Authored by RentReceipt.online Tax Editorial Team • Validated for FY 2025-26 & FY 2026-27 • Last Updated: November 4, 2026

While corporate salaried employees receiving House Rent Allowance (HRA) as part of their salary structure claim tax deductions under Section 10(13A), millions of Indian taxpayers do not have an HRA component in their income.

Freelancers, independent consultants, gig workers, small business proprietors, and salaried professionals working in organizations that do not offer HRA can still claim statutory tax deductions for rent paid under Section 80GG of the Income Tax Act, 1961.

Who is Eligible to Claim Section 80GG?

To claim deductions under Section 80GG, an individual taxpayer must satisfy three essential eligibility criteria:

  • Individual or HUF Status: You must be an Individual taxpayer or Hindu Undivided Family (HUF). Companies, LLPs, and partnership firms cannot claim Section 80GG.
  • No HRA Received During the Financial Year: Neither you nor your spouse, minor child, or HUF may receive any House Rent Allowance from an employer at any point during the relevant financial year.
  • No Self-Owned Residential Property: You, your spouse, your minor child, or your HUF must not own residential accommodation in the municipal limits of the town or city where you currently reside or perform employment/business duties. Furthermore, if you own residential property in another city, you cannot claim Section 80GG if you treat that distant property as self-occupied under Section 24.

The Three-Pronged Section 80GG Calculation Formula

The statutory deduction under Section 80GG is defined as the least (minimum) of the following three amounts:

Condition Statutory Basis Annual Cap
Condition 1: Fixed Statutory Ceiling ₹5,000 per month Maximum ₹60,000 per financial year
Condition 2: Percentage of Adjusted Income 25% of Total Adjusted Income Variable based on net earnings
Condition 3: Rent Paid Above Base Actual rent paid minus 10% of Total Adjusted Income Variable based on actual rent

Understanding 'Adjusted Total Income' for Section 80GG

In computing Section 80GG relief, Adjusted Total Income is defined as:

  • Gross Total Income (before Chapter VI-A deductions), minus:
  • Long-term capital gains (LTCG) taxed under Sections 112 or 112A.
  • Short-term capital gains (STCG) on equity shares taxed at 20% under Section 111A.
  • All deductions under Chapter VI-A (such as Sections 80C, 80D, 80E, 80TTA), excluding Section 80GG itself.

Worked Numerical Example

Consider Rohit, an independent IT consultant in Bengaluru earning an Adjusted Total Income of ₹8,00,000 per year. He pays ₹18,000 per month in rent (₹2,16,000 annually) for his residential flat. His Section 80GG deduction is computed as:

  • Condition 1: ₹5,000 × 12 = ₹60,000
  • Condition 2: 25% of ₹8,00,000 = ₹2,00,000
  • Condition 3: Actual Rent (₹2,16,000) − 10% of Income (₹80,000) = ₹1,36,000

The deduction allowed under Section 80GG is the lowest of the three: ₹60,000. At a 20% income tax slab, this deduction saves Rohit ₹12,480 in annual income tax.

Mandatory Online Filing of Form 10BA

To claim Section 80GG on your Income Tax Return, submitting Form 10BA is a mandatory statutory prerequisite. If you file your ITR claiming Section 80GG without submitting Form 10BA, the Centralized Processing Centre (CPC) will automatically issue a defective return notice under Section 139(9) or disallow the deduction.

How to Submit Form 10BA Online:

  1. Log in to the official Income Tax portal at eportal.incometax.gov.in using your PAN and password.
  2. Navigate to e-File → Income Tax Forms → File Income Tax Forms.
  3. Select Form 10BA (Declaration to be filed by an assessee claiming deduction under Section 80GG).
  4. Select the relevant Assessment Year and enter the rented premises address, landlord full name, landlord address, and landlord PAN (if rent exceeds ₹1 Lakh).
  5. E-verify the form using Aadhaar OTP or NetBanking before submitting your ITR-3 or ITR-4.
